FIRST AID INFORMATION

First Aid
Quick Reference

Simple first-aid instructions for common emergency situations. Use these steps as immediate guidance while arranging professional medical assistance.

Important: First aid does not replace professional medical care. In a serious or life-threatening situation, contact emergency services immediately.

Common Emergency Situations

Follow the appropriate steps and avoid actions that could make the situation worse.

01

Severe Bleeding

Heavy bleeding requires immediate attention. Act quickly while arranging emergency medical assistance.

What to do

  1. Call emergency medical services.
  2. Apply firm, direct pressure to the wound using clean cloth or gauze.
  3. Keep continuous pressure on the wound.
  4. If blood soaks through the material, add more material without removing the original layer.
  5. Keep the person as still and comfortable as possible.
Avoid Do not remove an object that is deeply embedded in the wound.
02

Choking

If a person cannot breathe, speak or cough effectively, immediate action may be required.

What to do

  1. Encourage the person to cough if they can cough effectively.
  2. If they cannot breathe or cough effectively, call emergency services.
  3. Give appropriate back blows and abdominal thrusts according to first-aid training.
  4. If the person becomes unresponsive, begin CPR and follow emergency dispatcher instructions.
Avoid Do not blindly put your fingers into the person's mouth to search for an object.
03

Burns

Cool minor thermal burns promptly and seek medical care for serious or extensive burns.

What to do

  1. Move away from the source of heat.
  2. Cool the affected area with cool running water for about 20 minutes.
  3. Remove jewellery or loose items near the affected area if they are not stuck.
  4. Cover the burn loosely with a clean, non-stick dressing or material.
Avoid Do not apply ice, butter, toothpaste or other household substances to a burn.
04

CPR & Cardiac Emergency

If a person is unresponsive and not breathing normally, treat the situation as an emergency.

What to do

  1. Check that the area is safe.
  2. Check responsiveness and normal breathing.
  3. Call emergency medical services and ask someone to get an AED if available.
  4. Begin chest compressions if the person is not breathing normally.
  5. Follow instructions from the emergency dispatcher and use an AED when available and appropriate.
Important If you are not trained in CPR, follow the emergency dispatcher's instructions.
05

Snakebite

A suspected venomous snakebite requires urgent medical evaluation.

What to do

  1. Move away from the snake and keep a safe distance.
  2. Call emergency medical services.
  3. Keep the affected person calm and still.
  4. Keep the bitten limb as still as possible.
  5. Remove rings, watches or tight items near the affected limb before swelling increases.
Avoid Do not cut or suck the wound, apply ice, or attempt to catch or handle the snake.
06

Heatstroke

Heatstroke is a medical emergency and can become life-threatening quickly.

What to do

  1. Call emergency medical services immediately.
  2. Move the person to a cool or shaded area.
  3. Remove unnecessary outer clothing.
  4. Cool the person rapidly using cool water, wet cloths, fans or other appropriate methods.
  5. Monitor the person until medical help arrives.
Important Do not delay emergency medical care when heatstroke is suspected.
GENERAL GUIDELINES

Before Providing First Aid

01. Check the scene

Make sure the area is safe before approaching an injured person.

02. Get help

Contact emergency services when the condition is serious or life-threatening.

03. Protect yourself

Use gloves or other protective barriers when available, especially when dealing with blood.

04. Stay calm

Speak calmly and reassure the injured person while waiting for professional help.

Is Someone in Immediate Danger?

Contact emergency services immediately for life-threatening situations.
